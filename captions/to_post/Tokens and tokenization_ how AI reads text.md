🧠 Techie Deep Dive:
Tokenization is the very first step in how a Large Language Model (LLM) processes text.

1.  What it is: It's the process of breaking down a piece of text into smaller units called tokens. A token can be a word, part of a word (a subword), or a single character.
2.  Why it's necessary: Computers don't understand words; they understand numbers. Each token is mapped to a unique integer ID. This sequence of integers is what is actually fed into the neural network.
3.  Subword Tokenization: Most modern LLMs use subword tokenization (e.g., using algorithms like Byte-Pair Encoding or WordPiece). This is a clever way to handle the vast vocabulary of human language. Common words might be a single token ("pizza"), while rare or complex words can be broken down into smaller, known subwords ("token" + "ization"). This allows the model to handle any word, even ones it has never seen before, and it keeps the vocabulary size manageable.

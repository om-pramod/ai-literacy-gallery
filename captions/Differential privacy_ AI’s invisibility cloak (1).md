🧠 Techie Deep Dive:
Differential Privacy is a system for publicly sharing information about a dataset by describing the patterns of groups within the dataset while withholding information about individuals. It provides a strong mathematical guarantee of privacy.

Here's the core idea: If you have a dataset and you run a query on it, the result of the query should not be significantly different if you remove any single individual from the dataset. This makes it impossible to learn anything specific about any one person. This is achieved by adding a carefully calibrated amount of "noise" to the query results. It's a powerful technique used by companies like Apple and Google and by government agencies like the US Census Bureau to release data without compromising individual privacy.

Browser cookies, on the other hand, are small files stored on your computer by websites you visit, often used for tracking your activity across the web for advertising purposes.
